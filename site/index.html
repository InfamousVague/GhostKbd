Ghostwriter and Mockingbird

AI that writes as you.

Type <<what you want>> in any app and close it with >>. Ghostwriter writes the answer where the request was. It reads what you have typed in the field around the request and, with Mockingbird, your own past messages, so the answer carries on from your draft and sounds like you.

You bring a key from Claude, OpenAI, Gemini or four other providers, or your own server, and pay them for what you use. There is no Ghost subscription and no Ghost server in between: what you ask about goes to the provider you chose.

Adaptive keys, autocorrect, swipe, dictation

A keyboard that learns your hands.

Every thumb has its habits: an i that lands on o, an a you reach short of. Switch on Adaptive keys and the keyboard learns where you really aim at each letter and moves the touch areas to meet you, by up to about a third of a key. Autocorrect catches the slips that still get through. If it gets one wrong, one backspace puts back exactly what you typed, and that word is left alone from then on.

You can see it

A Stats page draws your speed, the keys you miss and the words you put right most. A heatmap shows your slips and where each key now listens. One tap forgets it all. Adaptive keys is a switch in Settings › Typing, off to start, and the keyboard sends what it learns nowhere.

Six languages

English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese, with a programmer’s vocabulary and texting slang if you want it.

Swipe and dictation

Draw a word across the letters and lift. Add Dictation and whisper turns speech into text on the phone, in takes of up to 60 seconds.

Terminal keys, the { } page, markdown

Esc, Ctrl and Tab, on a phone.

A terminal needs Esc and Ctrl, and a phone keyboard has neither. Turn on Terminal keys in Settings › Row tools and Esc, Tab, sticky Ctrl and Alt, and the arrows show by themselves in Termux, Termius, JuiceSSH and ConnectBot, or in every app if you choose. The letters, the { } page and the rest of the keyboard stay as they are; the terminal keys sit in the row whenever you are not mid-word.

A { } page, a markdown row

Bracket and quote pairs with the caret left inside, arrows like =>, and function snippets. The markdown row is a switch in Settings › Row tools too, and then shows by itself in notes apps.

A programmer’s vocabulary

Autocorrect knows npm, async and Kotlin, and leaves code-shaped words alone. Undo and redo sit in the row.

No account, no server of ours

Nothing between you and your AI.

You paste a key from the provider you choose, and a request goes from your phone straight to that provider. The text you ask about, and the sources that are switched on, do go to them. Ghostwriter sends nothing until you close a request. Mockingbird sends samples of your writing to the same provider when it trains your style profile, which you can read and delete.

Themes, sounds, layouts

Ghost, Glacier, Midnight, Paper and Retro, or a theme from a colour you pick; six backlight effects; seven key sounds. Four key heights: Condensed takes about a fifth less of the screen. On an unfolded foldable or a tablet the keys split, fill the width or dock to a side; on a phone they can narrow toward one thumb.

Clipboard history, incognito

Your last 25 copies, a tap away, with no timer. Never what a password manager copied, and nothing at all while the incognito switch is on. With a switch you turn on, a sign-in code that arrives by text shows above the keys in a colour of its own, a tap to type it.

Emoji and GIFs

Emoji 18.0 with search and :shortcodes. GIF search goes to GIPHY, which sees what you search for.

Password-manager logins

Your manager’s logins in the row above the keys, drawn and filled by Android itself. Needs Android 11 or later.

Where Ghost Keyboard is behind

  • No next-word prediction yet
  • Six autocorrect languages
  • Dictation stops at 60 seconds, and shows text after you stop
  • No floating mode, stickers or handwriting
  • Accuracy not yet measured against Gboard or SwiftKey
  • The AI needs a key you get from a provider yourself
  • An APK, not a Play Store install

Questions.

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads, no subscription and no paid tier. The AI features use a key from a provider you choose, and you pay that provider for what you use. Everything else costs nothing.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no Ghost account. Typing, autocorrect, swipe, dictation, the clipboard and the terminal keys work without signing in to anything. Ghostwriter, Mockingbird and Rewrite need an API key from an AI provider, which is an account with them and not with us.

What leaves my phone?

Ordinary typing is not sent anywhere by the keyboard. What you type is sent somewhere only when you use one of these.

  • Ghostwriter. When you close a request, your AI provider gets the request, the text around it in the field (up to 1,500 characters before and 400 after), the app’s name, today’s date, your style profile, the past messages that share its words and your latest few from the same app, and the Ghost.md notes that match. Your clipboard goes only if you switch that on. With a GitHub token, the request’s keywords go to GitHub, and the commits found (date, repository, message) go to your AI provider with the request. Web lookups are your provider’s own search.
  • Mockingbird. A selection of your writing samples goes to your AI provider when it trains your style profile, and again when it retrains by itself, about once a day at most. No request has to be typed for that.
  • In my voice and Rewrite. The text you chose goes to your AI provider; In my voice sends your style profile and a few similar samples with it.
  • GIFs. Your search goes to GIPHY.
  • Dictation. Speech is turned into text on the phone. The network is used only to download the speech model, from ghostkbd.com or huggingface.co.
  • Updates. The app asks ghostkbd.com for the current version when it opens and up to twice a day.
  • Ghost.md online. If you have signed in to Ghost.md and no note on the phone matches a request, its keywords go to ghostmarkdown.com.
  • Android’s own backup. The app does not yet tell Android to leave its data out of backups. If backup or device transfer is on, Android may copy the app’s data, including your AI key, clipboard history and what the keyboard has learned.

There is no analytics or crash-reporting library in the app. That comes from reading the source, not from an outside audit.

Does it read my text messages?

Not unless you turn on one-time codes in Settings › Row tools, and Android asks you before it allows that. With it on, each text that arrives is looked at once, on the phone, for a sign-in code. Only the code is kept, in memory, for ten minutes or until you use it. The text itself and who sent it are not kept, and nothing of it is sent anywhere.

The app cannot read the messages already on your phone and cannot send any: it asks Android only to receive new ones. With the switch off, Android does not hand texts to the app at all. How it works, on GitHub.

Which AI providers can I use?

Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, Mistral, Groq, DeepSeek and OpenRouter, or any OpenAI-compatible server of your own whose address starts with https://. You pick the provider and the model, and you can change either. Web lookups work with Claude, OpenAI, Gemini and OpenRouter, which have a search of their own.

Why is it an APK, and is that safe?

Ghost Keyboard is not on Google Play yet, so you install the APK from this page, and Android asks you to allow that first. A keyboard sees what you type, so hold it to a high standard: the source is public on GitHub, there is no account, and there is no analytics or crash-reporting library in the app.

The download’s size and SHA-256 are published beside the button so you can check the file you got. An update is installed only if it matches the published size and SHA-256 and is signed with the same certificate as the copy already on your phone. Any app installed this way asks you to trust its publisher; we would rather you could check than take our word.

Is it on iOS or Google Play?

Not today. Ghost Keyboard is Android only, for Android 8 or later, and is not on Google Play, F-Droid or the App Store. You install it from this page and it keeps itself up to date.

Which languages does it support?

Autocorrect covers English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese, and several can be on at once. Dictation starts with an English model; models that hear about a hundred languages can be downloaded in its settings. The letters are QWERTY.

Is it open source?

The source is public on GitHub, so you can read what the keyboard does. The repository has no licence yet, so it is not open source in the usual sense of the words.

Get Ghost Keyboard.

Your keys. Your model. Your words. Free, on Android 8 or later.

  1. Download the APK and open it. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser first.
  2. Turn it on. Open Ghost Keyboard and tap the first step, which opens your phone’s keyboard list.
  3. Switch to it. Tap the second step and choose Ghost Keyboard. That is all.

It keeps itself up to date. The app checks ghostkbd.com when it opens and up to twice a day, and installs an update only if the file matches the published size and SHA-256 and is signed with the same certificate as the copy you have.
